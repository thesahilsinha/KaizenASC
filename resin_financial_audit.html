02 Pricing Analysis

The surface-level pricing picture is actually healthier than expected — a 93.3% average realization rate suggests Shreyash is not heavily discounting on standard deals. But the floor tells a different story.

Deal range — listed price vs. average realised vs. lowest recorded
What's working: ₹42K ÷ ₹45K = 93.3% realization. That's a healthy close rate on standard engagements. Discounting is selective, not habitual.
What's broken: The ₹25K floor deal represents a 44% discount — nearly half-price. If this happened even once without a documented strategic reason (portfolio anchor, referral reward, relationship investment), it sets a dangerous precedent internally. The fix: define a minimum acceptable price and never go below it without a written rationale.
Current pricing structure
Single flat rate (₹45K) with informal negotiation downward. No documented tiers. Discount decisions made in the moment without a framework.
Recommended structure
Three named tiers — Standard (₹45K), Growth (₹75K), and Scale (₹1L+) — with clearly defined scopes. Discounts only applied to Standard, never below ₹35K floor.

06 Profitability Status

The single most important insight from this entire audit. When asked directly, the response on both gross profit and gross margin was: "Not sure."

What Resin knows vs. what it needs to know to scale confidently
What "Not sure" means in practice: Every decision about hiring, pricing increases, whether to take on a lower-value project, or whether to invest in ads is currently being made without knowing if the business is making or losing money on each engagement. This is the financial equivalent of driving at night without headlights — momentum exists, but the risks are invisible.
07 Six Red Flags

A consolidated view of the structural issues surfaced by this audit, ordered by urgency.

Revenue inconsistency — 3 clients × ₹42K ≠ ₹40K/month
The most urgent item. This gap must be explained before any other financial analysis is trustworthy. Likely explanation: one active client per month, or ₹40K is per-client not total.
Profitability unknown — gross profit and gross margin both unanswered
Without knowing margin per project, every scale decision is a guess. This needs to be the first number to establish.
Founder labor uncosted — payroll reported as ₹0
Understates true delivery cost. Inflates apparent margin. Sets a false baseline for profitability discussions.
Contractor costs omitted despite acknowledged part-timer dependency
Development relies on part-timers, yet contractor cost = ₹0. These two statements cannot both be true simultaneously.
No service-level financial breakdown
All financial data references website design only. Three other listed services have zero tracked revenue, cost, or margin data.
₹25K floor deal — 44% discount with no documented rationale
Selective deep discounting without a framework creates pricing precedents that are hard to reverse and signal low price confidence to future clients.
08 Operational Bottleneck

Shreyash correctly identifies sales — specifically lead generation and conversion — as the primary operational constraint. This is likely accurate. But there is a second question that needs to be answered before investing in sales.

Stated bottleneck
"Sales. Generate leads and convert."
This is a real and accurate constraint. Solving it is the right priority once financials are clarified.
The question to answer first
"If you signed 10 new website projects next month, could your current setup deliver all of them on time?"

If the answer is no, sales is not the only bottleneck — delivery capacity is a co-constraint that needs to be solved in parallel.
A business that sells faster than it can deliver doesn't grow — it damages its reputation and loses the clients it just acquired. Validate delivery capacity before accelerating acquisition.
09 30-Day Financial Clarity Roadmap

Before any growth investment — paid ads, outreach tools, hiring — these financial foundations must be in place. This is a 30-day sprint, not a 90-day programme.

Week 1 — Resolve the numbers
Days 1–7
Reconcile revenue: how many active clients per month, actual total collected
List every contractor paid in last 3 months with amounts
Assign a founder hourly rate — even an estimate (₹500–₹1,000/hr)
Calculate hours per project, multiply by rate = true delivery cost
Week 2 — Build the unit economics
Days 8–14
Calculate gross profit per project: revenue minus fully loaded cost
Calculate gross margin %: gross profit ÷ revenue × 100
Separate financials for website design vs. other services
Identify which services are active vs. listed-only
Week 3–4 — Systemise and standardise
Days 15–30
Set minimum acceptable price floor (recommend ₹35K)
Create pricing tier document: Standard / Growth / Scale
Set up the monthly dashboard (see Section 10)
Decide on development: retain with better contractor network, or pause

The financial truth
Resin has strong technical capability and a founder who is already closing deals and running active outreach. What's missing is the financial layer that makes growth legible. Right now, decisions about pricing, hiring, and investment are made by feel rather than by data. That is not a character flaw — it is simply a stage of business. The 30-day clarity sprint exists to close that gap permanently. Once the numbers are real, every decision the business makes becomes sharper, faster, and easier to defend.
